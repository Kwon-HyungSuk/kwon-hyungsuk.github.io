Ultrasound Sensing

Modular CNN for Adaptive Ultrasound Sensing (2021/1 - 2023/11)

A modular convolutional neural network (CNN) was developed for object identification and localization through echo analysis. The model achieved excellent performance even when echoes from multiple objects interfered with each other. Additionally, the architecture can be easily expanded to incorporate new object classes after training. The results were published in IEEE Access (2025).

Ultrasound Beam Steering Metamaterial Lens (2019/1 - 2020/12)

A reconfigurable acoustic metamaterial lens was developed for ultrasound beamforming and steering. The metamaterial lens uses a single actuated element to manipulate its gradient-index profile and thereby control the direction of transmitted ultrasound beams. The device was experimentally demonstrated by steering an ultrasound beam up to 15°. The results were published in Smart Materials and Structures (2021).

Pressure Enhancing Passive Metafluid (2017/5 - 2018/12)

An acoustic pressure-enhancing passive metafluid was developed to improve the sensitivity of MEMS microphones. The metafluid leverages the influence of acoustic impedance on sound pressure and particle velocity to increase the pressure of incoming sound waves in a broadband manner. The results were published in The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America (2019).

Ultrasound Energy Transfer

Metamaterial for Ultrasound Energy Transfer (2024/1 - 2025/12)

A flexible ultrasound energy harvester was developed for medical and underwater applications. Its performance was enhanced using a sound-focusing metamaterial composed of air cavities, making the device flexible and lightweight. The results were published in Advanced Materials (2026).

Underwater Acoustics
Underwater acoustics addresses how acoustic signals propagate, scatter, and interact in marine environments. My research in this direction aims to develop acoustic methods for communication, sensing, and energy transfer in underwater systems where electromagnetic signals are ineffective or impractical.
